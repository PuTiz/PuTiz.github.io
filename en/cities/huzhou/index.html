City Intro

History & Culture: Huzhou is in Zhejiang and is known for destinations such as Xiaolianzhuang; it is an important travel hub in the region. Since the Tang and Song dynasties, Huzhou has been a centre of sericulture, grain transport and learning in the Lake Tai basin, while Nanxun grew wealthy on silk. Huzhou lies in Zhejiang and has formed a distinctive urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls record historical layers from different periods. Today museums, historic districts and local festivals still make the city's history legible. The city's history is written not only in sites and museums but also in dialect, food, festivals and the details of daily life.

Geography: Huzhou's core landscapes include Xiaolianzhuang, Zhang Shiming Residence, Baijianlou and Jiayetang Library, with nature and culture existing side by side within a short distance. Huzhou lies in Zhejiang, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's spatial pattern. March–May and September–November are best; Nanxun's water town is even more evocative in the rain. Light, vegetation and water levels change the look of the landscape with the seasons; check the weather and official notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and the main sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Xiaolianzhuang

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

The private garden of Liu Yong, the richest man in Nanxun; the lotus pond, archways and library combine Jiangnan gardens with merchant culture. Highlights include the Liu family ancestral temple and Dongsheng Pavilion. Tip: The combo ticket covers Xiaolianzhuang and Jiayetang; the lotus is most beautiful in summer. Practical info: located at Nanxun Town, Nanxun District, Huzhou, Zhejiang; ticket ¥100 (Nanxun old-town combo ticket); opening hours 08:00–17:00. Best season: March–May and September–November; Nanxun's water town is even more evocative in the rain. Baijianlou

Baijianlou

★★★★☆Recommend 4.5 / 5

A Ming-era residential group built along the river, where more than a hundred houses and arcades form a continuous row — the most lived-in stretch of Nanxun. Highlights include the riverside houses, stone bridges and morning mist. Tip: Go early in the morning for the mist and the people washing clothes; no ticket is needed. Practical info: located at Nanxun Town, Nanxun District, Huzhou, Zhejiang; ticket free; opening hours all day. Culture

Huzhou is a key source of Hubi brushes, silk and tea culture. Hubi brush-making, the mulberry-and-silk traditions of Nanxun, Huju opera and tea rituals carry on the Jiangnan way of life on the shores of Lake Tai.

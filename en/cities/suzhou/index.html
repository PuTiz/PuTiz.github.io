City Intro

History & Culture: Suzhou was founded in 514 BC and is a national historical and cultural city with more than 2,500 years of history. It has long been praised in the saying 'Above there is heaven, below there are Suzhou and Hangzhou'. Suzhou is known worldwide for its classical gardens, which embody the art of creating a universe within a few paces. Wu culture is refined and elegant: Kunqu opera is melodious, pingtan storytelling uses the soft Wu dialect, and Su embroidery is exquisite. Suzhou blends the classical and the modern, with both the restrained elegance of its gardens and the vitality of a modern city. Strolling the water lanes and listening to a piece of pingtan is the finest way to experience the gentle spirit of Jiangnan. Suzhou lies in Jiangsu, where regional history and cultural exchange have shaped a distinct urban memory.

Geography: Suzhou lies in the middle of the Yangtze River Delta on the shores of Lake Tai, bordering Shanghai to the east. Waterways and canals crisscross the area, and the climate is subtropical monsoon. Suzhou is in Jiangsu, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's layout. Spring (March–May) brings flowers to the gardens; autumn (September–November) is crisp and clear. Natural and cultural sights are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary by area works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and official notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and the main sights can be linked from the city or county town. Natural and cultural sights around the city can usually be linked by road or rail, suiting a half- or full-day plan.

Highlights: Visit World Heritage gardens such as the Humble Administrator's Garden and the Lingering Garden, stroll Pingjiang Road and Shantang Street for water-town atmosphere, see the leaning pagoda at Tiger Hill and hear the bell at Hanshan Temple. Core sights include the Humble Administrator's Garden, Tiger Hill, Pingjiang Road Historic District and Suzhou Museum. They cover history, nature and city life and can be combined freely. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or natural areas as time allows.

Food Culture: Suzhou cuisine is refined and elegant: squirrel-shaped mandarin fish is crisp outside and tender within, Suzhou-style noodles are fine with a clear broth, crab-shell pastries are flaky and fragrant, plum blossom cakes are sweet and glutinous, and Yangcheng Lake hairy crab is full of roe and fat in autumn.

Humble Administrator's Garden

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

The Humble Administrator's Garden is one of China's four great gardens. First built during the Ming Zhengde reign, it covers 5.2 hectares. Water is the centre of the whole garden, with hills and streams winding through, exquisite halls and pavilions, and lush flowers and trees. Highlights include the Hall of Distant Fragrance, the Little Flying Rainbow bridge, the Thirty-Six Mandarin Ducks Hall and the Listening to the Rain Pavilion. One of China's four great gardens and a World Heritage Site, it arranges its pavilions around water in a spacious, natural way and is a model of Jiangnan garden design. Tip: go when the garden opens in the morning for fewer people and finer views. Practical info: the garden is at 178 Dongbei Street, Gusu District, Suzhou, Jiangsu; ticket ¥80 in peak season and ¥70 off-peak; opening hours 07:30–17:30 (peak) and 07:30–17:00 (off-peak). Best season: spring (March–May) brings flowers to the gardens; autumn (September–November) is crisp and clear. Combine with Tiger Hill, Pingjiang Road Historic District and Suzhou Museum for a half- or full-day route. Confirm the day's opening and reservation policy before you go and allow ample time.

Tip: Go when the garden opens in the morning for fewer people and finer views.

  • Ticket: Peak season ¥80 / off-peak ¥70
  • Opening: 07:30–17:30 (peak season); 07:30–17:00 (off-peak)

Tiger Hill

★★★★☆Recommend 4.6 / 5

Tiger Hill is Suzhou's landmark sight, with more than 2,500 years of history. The Yunyan Temple Pagoda was begun in 959, the sixth year of the Later Zhou Xiande era, and completed in 961, the second year of the Northern Song Jianlong era; from the Ming dynasty its top leaned to the northwest, and modern reinforcement has kept it standing despite the tilt, earning it the name Leaning Tower of Pisa of the East. Highlights include the Sword Pool, the Thousand-Person Rock and the Sword-Testing Stone. Known as the finest sight in Wu, it has a pagoda that has leaned for a thousand years, called the Leaning Tower of Pisa of the East, and the legends of the Sword Pool are captivating. Tip: enter by the south gate and follow the central axis. Practical info: the site is at 8 inside Tiger Hill Gate, Gusu District, Suzhou, Jiangsu; ticket ¥70 in peak season and ¥60 off-peak; opening hours 07:30–17:30. Best season: spring (March–May) brings flowers to the gardens; autumn (September–November) is crisp and clear. Combine with the Humble Administrator's Garden, Pingjiang Road Historic District and Suzhou Museum for a half- or full-day route. Plan your stay according to opening hours and your energy.

Tip: Enter by the south gate and follow the central axis. The Sword Pool is the most mysterious spot on Tiger Hill.

  • Address: 8 inside Tiger Hill Gate, Gusu District, Suzhou, Jiangsu
  • Ticket: Peak season ¥70 / off-peak ¥60
  • Opening: 07:30–17:30

Pingjiang Road Historic District

★★★★☆Recommend 4.5 / 5

Pingjiang Road is Suzhou's best-preserved old street, 1,606 metres long and built along the Pingjiang River, retaining the water-town pattern of land and water running side by side with the canal next to the street. Highlights include the Kunqu Opera Museum, the Pingtan Museum, the Couple's Retreat Garden and the cluster of old bridges. A well-preserved old water lane with canal and street in parallel and old bridges and houses in succession, it offers the authentic slow life of old Suzhou and the flavour of Wu. Tip: go in the afternoon, when you can hear snatches of pingtan and experience Suzhou's slow pace. Practical info: the district is on Pingjiang Road, Gusu District, Suzhou, Jiangsu; admission free; open all day. Best season: spring (March–May) brings flowers to the gardens; autumn (September–November) is crisp and clear. Combine with the Humble Administrator's Garden, Tiger Hill and Suzhou Museum for a half- or full-day route. Confirm the day's opening and reservation policy before you go and allow ample time. In peak season or on holidays, book in advance, travel off-peak and allow time for queues and security checks.

Tip: Go in the afternoon, when you can hear snatches of pingtan and experience Suzhou's slow pace.

  • Address: Pingjiang Road, Gusu District, Suzhou, Jiangsu
  • Ticket: Free
  • Opening: Open all day

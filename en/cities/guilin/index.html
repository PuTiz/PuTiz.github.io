City Intro

History & Culture: Guilin has over 2,000 years of history since being established as a commandery during the Qin Dynasty. The renowned phrase "Guilin's scenery is the finest under heaven" has echoed through millennia. Cliff inscriptions line the Li River banks, the Jingjiang Prince's Mansion recounts Ming Dynasty tales, and the Lingqu Canal — a Qin-era engineering marvel linking the Yangtze and Pearl River systems — witnesses the early fusion of Central Plains and Lingnan cultures. Guilin's landscape has nurtured a distinctive folk-song culture and multi-ethnic customs. At the Zhuang "March Third" song fair, young men and women sing to express love and to choose partners, and the legend of Liu Sanjie is the artistic embodiment of this tradition. Ethnic handicrafts such as Zhuang brocade and Yao embroidery are exquisite, and the Dong Grand Chorus is renowned worldwide for its polyphonic singing without a conductor or accompaniment. Cliff inscriptions along the banks of the Li River have continued without a break since the Tang and Song, recording the literati's praises of the landscape. The Jingjiang Princes' City carries the culture of Ming princely fiefs, and the famous line "Guilin's scenery is the finest under heaven" was first carved on the rock face of Solitary Beauty Peak. Guilin blends natural beauty with cultural charm, creating the distinctive experience of "travelling within a painting."

Geography: A quintessential karst landscape where the Li River winds like a jade ribbon through the city. Limestone peaks rise dramatically from the earth, caves display endless formations, and a warm, humid subtropical climate with abundant rainfall has painted one of the world's most celebrated landscape scrolls. Guilin lies in Guangxi, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's spatial pattern. Spring and autumn (April–May, September–November) have moderate rainfall, when the Li River scenery is at its clearest and most beautiful. Natural and cultural sites are relatively concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary by area works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change the look of the landscape with the seasons; check the weather and scenic-area notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and major sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Highlights: Float the Li River by bamboo raft, see the iconic silhouette of Elephant Trunk Hill, wander the international-flavoured West Street in Yangshuo and climb to the Longji terraces to see farming among the clouds — like entering an ink-wash painting. Core sights include the Li River, Elephant Trunk Hill, Jingjiang Princes' City (Solitary Beauty Peak) and Yangshuo West Street. They cover history, nature and city life, and can be combined freely. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or nature as time allows.

Food Culture: Guilin rice noodles are silky and fragrant with rich braised-meat toppings — every local's top breakfast choice. Beer fish is tender and flavorful, Lipu taro pork is soft and savory, and the flavors of northern Guangxi shine uniquely against this backdrop of mountains and waters.

Li River

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

Called Guishui in antiquity, the Li rises on Mount Mao'er in Xing'an County, whose 2,141.5-metre summit is hailed as the highest peak in southern China; below Pingle the stream takes the name Gui Jiang, reaches the Xi River at Wuzhou and so drains into the Pearl River system, while the 83-kilometre stretch from Guilin to Yangshuo has long been treated as the scenic core. At its 38th session in 2014 the UNESCO World Heritage Committee inscribed Guilin karst as part of the second phase of South China Karst, and the dense forest of tower-like karst hills along the banks is the landscape that earned it. According to the Li River scenic area's own website, the cruise route begins at Mopanshan passenger port, under 30 km from downtown Guilin, and ends at Longtoushan wharf in Yangshuo, covering about 61 kilometres. Highlights include the 20-yuan banknote viewpoint. Tip: the Yangdi–Xingping bamboo raft is recommended, about 1.5–2 hours. Practical info: located on the Li River in Guilin (the Guilin–Yangshuo section, with Yangdi–Xingping as the highlight); tickets vary by raft/cruise; opening hours 08:00–17:30.

Tip: Yangdi to Xingping bamboo raft (about 1.5–2 hours) is recommended.

  • Address: Li River, Guilin to Yangshuo (highlight section Yangdi–Xingping)
  • Ticket: Varies by raft/cruise
  • Opening: 08:00–17:30
Elephant Trunk Hill

Elephant Trunk Hill

★★★★☆Recommend 4.5 / 5

Elephant Trunk Hill resembles a giant elephant dipping its trunk to drink from the Li River, with the rounded Water Moon Cave formed between trunk and body; it is Guilin's city emblem. Highlights include Puxian Pagoda, Elephant Eye Cave and the confluence of the Li and Taohua rivers. It is a natural rock hill that looks like a giant elephant stretching its trunk to drink the Li River, with the rounded Water Moon Cave between trunk and body. On moonlit nights, the reflection in the cave and on the water create the poetic image of "a bright moon at the bottom of the water, a bright moon floating on the water." On the hill stands the Ming-dynasty Puxian Pagoda, and the summit looks over the beautiful confluence of the Li and Taohua rivers — the most classic calling card of Guilin's scenery. Tip: the silhouette at dusk is most moving. Practical info: located on Binjiang Road, Xiangshan District, Guilin; admission free (all charges dropped from January 2022); opening hours 07:00–21:30. Best season: spring and autumn (April–May, September–November) have moderate rainfall, when the Li River scenery is at its clearest and most beautiful. Pair it with the Li River, Jingjiang Princes' City (Solitary Beauty Peak) and Yangshuo West Street for a half- or full-day itinerary.

Tip: The silhouette at dusk is most evocative.

  • Address: Binjiang Road, Xiangshan District
  • Ticket: Free (admission charge scrapped in January 2022)
  • Opening: 07:00–21:30

Jingjiang Princes' City (Solitary Beauty Peak)

Jingjiang Princes' City (Solitary Beauty Peak)

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

The Jingjiang Princes' City was first built in 1372, the fifth year of the Ming Hongwu reign, as the residence of Zhu Shouqian, Prince of Jingjiang, and it is the best-preserved Ming princely palace in China. Highlights include Solitary Beauty Peak, the inscription of the famous line, the cliff inscriptions and Chengyun Gate with the palace platform. At its heart rises Solitary Beauty Peak, where the line "Guilin's scenery is the finest under heaven" was first carved into the cliff face, and nearly 200 inscriptions from successive dynasties still cover its rocks, layering princely history and literati verse on one mountain. The walled city now lies inside the Wangcheng campus of Guangxi Normal University — red walls set against the sound of study, a rare national 5A scenic area within a university — and the summit gives a panorama of all Guilin. Tip: the summit is the best spot to look over the whole city; go in the morning and allow 2 hours; the site overlaps with the university campus, so keep quiet when passing teaching areas. Practical info: located at No. 1 Wangcheng Road, Xiufeng District, Guilin; tickets from ¥89 (includes the climb up Solitary Beauty Peak; combo tickets and guide services per notices on site); opening hours 07:30–18:30.

Tip: The peak gives the best view over the whole city — go in the morning and allow about two hours.

  • Address: No. 1 Wangcheng Road, Xiufeng District
  • Ticket: From ¥89 (includes the climb up Solitary Beauty Peak; combo tickets per notice on site)
  • Opening: 07:30–18:30

Culture

Guilin's mountains and waters have nurtured a rich tradition of folk song culture and multi-ethnic customs. The Zhuang people's "March Third" Song Festival features young men and women singing to express love and choose partners — the legend of Third Sister Liu (Liu Sanjie) is the artistic embodiment of this tradition. Zhuang brocade and Yao embroidery are exquisite handicrafts, while the Dong Grand Chorus — a polyphonic choir performed without conductor or accompaniment — has earned worldwide acclaim. Cliff inscriptions along the Li River stretch unbroken from the Tang and Song dynasties, recording poets' and scholars' odes to the landscape. The Jingjiang Prince's Mansion carries Ming Dynasty royal culture, and the famous line "Guilin's scenery is the finest under heaven" was first carved on Duxiu Peak's stone. Guilin perfectly fuses natural beauty with humanistic charm, achieving the unique experience of "walking within a painting."

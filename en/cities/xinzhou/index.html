City Intro

History & Culture: Xinzhou is the gateway to northern Shanxi; Wutai Shan has been a Buddhist sacred site since the Northern Wei, and the temple cluster at Taihuai together with Foguang Temple and Nanchan Temple forms a treasure house of China's early timber architecture. Wutai Shan is the foremost of China's four great Buddhist mountains, where Chinese and Tibetan Buddhism coexist; the East Hall of Foguang Temple was built in the eleventh year of the Dazhong era of the Tang (857) and was called “China's first national treasure” by Liang Sicheng. Xinzhou is located in Shanxi Province and has formed a distinctive urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls from different periods remain as historical layers. Today museums, historic districts and local festivals still make the city's historical thread legible.

Geography: The Taihang Mountains stand to the east and the Yellow River lies to the west; Wutai Shan is ringed by five peaks, and Taihuai Town, at about 1,700 metres, is the centre for summer retreats and pilgrimages around the peaks. Xinzhou is located in Shanxi Province, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's spatial pattern. June–September is the most comfortable, with cool summers on Wutai Shan; winters are cold with snow and ice on the peaks and require professional equipment. Natural and cultural sights are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary can be arranged by area. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and official scenic-area notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and the main sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Highlights: Make a pilgrimage around the peaks, see Foguang Temple's Tang timber architecture, eat vegetarian food in Taihuai, then go to Yanmen Pass to see the Great Wall. Core sights include Wuye Temple, Tayuan Temple, Xiantong Temple and Pusading. These sights cover different sides of history, nature and city life and can be combined freely according to interest. It is worth linking the core sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half-day or full-day itinerary. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest for a fuller experience. First-time visitors can start with the most representative core sights, then add museums, old streets or natural areas as time allows. Local food is part of the itinerary too — save mealtime for a time-honoured shop or a local market.

Food Culture: Tai mushroom, vegetarian temple food, oat noodles and Xinzhou wa-su pastries reflect the interplay of mountain and monastic cooking.

Wuye Temple

Wuye Temple

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

The temple with the most incense smoke on Wutai Shan, dedicated to the Dragon King Bodhisattva “Wuye”; the early-morning queue to offer incense is the liveliest scene in Taihuai. Highlights include the Wanfo Pavilion, the Wuye statue, early-morning incense offerings and the centre of Taihuai. Tip: The crowds are largest at 5–6 in the morning; if you want to look at the architecture in peace, go in the evening; loud noise is not allowed in the temple. Practical info: located at Taihuai Town, Wutai County, Xinzhou, Shanxi; ticket included in the Wutai Shan entrance fee (small temple tickets extra); opening hours 06:00–18:00. Best season: June–September is the most comfortable, with cool summers on Wutai Shan; winters are cold with snow and ice on the peaks and require professional equipment. It can be combined with Tayuan Temple, Xiantong Temple and Pusading in Xinzhou for a half-day or full-day itinerary. Check the day's opening and booking policy before setting out, and allow plenty of time for the visit. In peak season or on holidays, book ahead, travel off-peak and allow time for queues and security checks. Plan your length of stay around the opening hours and your energy.

  • Address: Taihuai Town, Wutai County, Xinzhou, Shanxi
  • Ticket: Included in the Wutai Shan entrance fee (small temple tickets extra)
  • Opening: 06:00–18:00

Tayuan Temple

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Home of the Great White Pagoda, the landmark of Taihuai; the pagoda is about 75 metres tall and is the symbol of Wutai Shan, and circling it is an important Tibetan Buddhist custom. Highlights include the sutra library. Tip: Circle the pagoda clockwise, never anticlockwise; the pagoda courtyard is next to Xiantong Temple, so the two can be seen together. Practical info: located at Taihuai Town, Wutai County, Xinzhou, Shanxi; ticket included in the entrance fee (small tickets extra); opening hours 06:30–18:00. Xiantong Temple

Xiantong Temple

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

One of the oldest temples on Wutai Shan, founded in the Eastern Han; the bronze hall, Wuliang Hall and Thousand-Bowl Manjushri are the highlights. Highlights include the axis of the ancient temple. Tip: The bronze hall is very bright in sunlight, so morning is good for photographs; flash is not allowed inside the halls.

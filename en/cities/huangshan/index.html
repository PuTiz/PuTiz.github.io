City Intro

History & Culture: Huangshan is famed for its five wonders — odd pines, grotesque rocks, sea of clouds, hot springs and winter snow — and for the saying 'No need to look at other mountains after the Five Sacred Mountains; no need to look at the Five Sacred Mountains after Huangshan.' The ancient Huizhou villages of Hongcun and Xidi at its foot are World Cultural Heritage sites, their white walls, dark tiles and stepped horse-head walls earning the name 'villages in a Chinese painting'. Huizhou culture has deep roots: the Xin'an Painting School, Hui-style architecture, Hui cuisine, Hui ink and She inkstones all originate here. Hui-style architecture is distinguished by white walls, dark tiles and horse-head walls, with exquisite brick, wood and stone carvings. The Xin'an Painting School stands apart, and Hui ink and She inkstones are famed throughout the world. Hui cuisine is one of China's eight great cuisines, emphasising generous oil, rich colour and strong heat. Huizhou people value scholarship and education, with a strong clan culture; ancestral halls and memorial archways are scattered across the countryside. The annual Huangshan International Mountaineering Festival draws many climbing enthusiasts, and intangible heritage such as Huizhou folk performances and fish-lantern dances is still passed down today.

Geography: Located in southern Anhui, in the mountainous south of the province, it is a World Cultural and Natural Heritage site and a Global Geopark. The main peak, Lotus Peak, rises 1,864 metres, with peaks competing in beauty and the Welcoming Pine known throughout the world. It combines the grandeur of Mount Tai, the steepness of Mount Hua, the mist of Mount Heng and the waterfalls of Mount Lu. Huangshan City lies in Anhui, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's layout. Spring (Mar–May) offers a high chance of seeing the sea of clouds, autumn (Sep–Nov) turns the forests red and gold, and winter (Dec–Feb) brings rime ice and snow. Natural and cultural sites are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary by area works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and scenic-area notices before setting out.

Highlights: Climb Huangshan for the sea of clouds and sunrise, admire the Welcoming Pine and explore the West Sea Grand Canyon; wander Hongcun and Xidi for the old Huizhou atmosphere; and taste stinky mandarin fish and hairy tofu for the true flavours of Hui cuisine. Core sights include Huangshan Scenic Area, Hongcun, Huizhou Ancient City and Xidi. They cover history, nature and city life, and can be combined freely to suit your interests. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or natural areas as time permits. Local food is part of the trip too — save a meal for a time-honoured shop or a local market.

Food Culture: Hui cuisine is one of China's eight great cuisines, known for generous oil, rich colour and strong heat. Stinky mandarin fish smells pungent but tastes fragrant; hairy tofu is crisp outside and tender within; Huangshan sesame cakes are flaky and layered; and yipin guo simmers meat and vegetables together, showing the plain, hearty character of Huizhou cooking.

Huangshan Scenic Area

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

Huangshan Scenic Area is a World Cultural and Natural Heritage site, famed for its five wonders: odd pines, grotesque rocks, sea of clouds, hot springs and winter snow. The main peak, Lotus Peak, rises 1,864 metres, with peaks competing in beauty and ever-changing weather. The Welcoming Pine, with its graceful form, has become a symbol of Chinese hospitality. Highlights include Bright Summit, Tiandu Peak and the West Sea Grand Canyon. Famous for its odd pines, grotesque rocks, sea of clouds, hot springs and winter snow, it offers spectacular sights such as the Welcoming Pine, Bright Summit, Tiandu Peak and the West Sea Grand Canyon; from the summits you can watch the sunrise over the sea of clouds — the foremost of all wondrous mountains. Tip: it is recommended to ascend by the front mountain and descend by the back mountain, with cable car assistance available. Hongcun

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Founded in the Southern Song dynasty, Hongcun is laid out in the shape of an ox: Moon Marsh is the stomach, South Lake the belly and the water channels the intestines. White-walled, dark-tiled Hui-style buildings are reflected in Moon Marsh and South Lake, forming a natural ink-wash painting and earning the name 'village in a Chinese painting'. Highlights include Chengzhi Hall and the Hui-style building cluster. A World Cultural Heritage site and a model Hui-style ancient village, Hongcun is shaped like an ox, with Moon Marsh and South Lake reflecting white walls and dark tiles; it is known as a 'village in a Chinese painting' and a living fossil of Huizhou architecture. Tip: light is most beautiful at dawn and dusk, ideal for photography. Practical info: located at Hongcun Town, Yi County, Huangshan City, Anhui; ticket ¥104; opening hours 07:30–17:30. Best season: spring (Mar–May) offers a high chance of seeing the sea of clouds; autumn (Sep–Nov) turns the forests red and gold; winter (Dec–Feb) brings rime ice and snow. Combine with Huangshan Scenic Area, Xidi and Tunxi Old Street in Huangshan for a half-day or full-day itinerary. Check the day's opening and reservation rules before you go, and allow enough time to explore.

Huizhou Ancient City

★★★★★Recommend 4.6 / 5

Huizhou Ancient City lies in Huicheng Town, Shexian County. Since Shexian was established in the Qin dynasty it has been the seat of Huizhou prefecture, and it is a relatively well-preserved prefectural and county-level ancient city. The Xu Guo Archway stands at its centre, linked with the Huizhou Prefecture Office, Hui Garden, Doushan Street, the Xu Guo residence and the Tao Xingzhi Memorial; outside the city, Yuliang Dam is one of the oldest dams on the upper Xin'an River and the wharf from which Huizhou merchants set sail. Compared with the village landscapes of Hongcun and Xidi, it emphasises the complete structure of prefecture, clan and merchant guild. The seat of the old Huizhou prefecture and, with Lijiang, Pingyao and Langzhong, one of China's four great ancient cities, it links the Xu Guo Archway, Hui Garden, Doushan Street and Yuliang Dam into a prefectural layout — more of a city than Hongcun or Xidi, and the place to see the Huizhou prefecture office and archways. Tip: combine with other Shexian sights on the same day; see the prefecture office and archway first, then Yuliang Dam. The old city is walkable, and combo ticket details are subject to on-site notice. Practical info: located at Huicheng Town, Shexian County, Huangshan; ticket about ¥80 (combo ticket including the Xu Guo Archway and Hui Garden; subject to official notice); opening hours 08:00–17:30.

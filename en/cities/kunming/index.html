City Intro

History & Culture: Since ancient times Kunming has been an important node on the 'Southern Silk Road' and the Tea Horse Road, with many ethnic groups living together and a deep historical and cultural heritage. Kunming is the capital of Yunnan, home to many ethnic groups, with lively festivals such as the Yi Torch Festival and the Dai Water-Splashing Festival. Local opera forms such as Yunnan flower-lantern opera and Dian opera are distinctive. As the 'Spring City', Kunming has flowers all year, and flower culture is woven into daily life. Tea Horse Road culture and Southern Silk Road culture meet here, forming an open and inclusive urban character. Kunming lies in Yunnan and has formed a distinctive urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls record historical layers from different periods.

Geography: Kunming is the capital of Yunnan, located in the central Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau on the bank of Dianchi Lake. The city sits at about 1,890 metres, with a mild, humid climate — no fierce heat in summer, no severe cold in winter — earning it the name 'Spring City'. Kunming lies in Yunnan, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's layout. Spring-like all year, any time works, but spring (Mar–May) has the most flowers and autumn (Sep–Nov) the most comfortable weather. Natural and cultural sites are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary by area works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and scenic-area notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main transport, and major sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Highlights: Dianchi Lake stretches vast and misty, the Sleeping Beauty of the Western Hills lies along its shore, the Stone Forest is a work of nature, and Yunnan Ethnic Village showcases the cultures of 25 ethnic minorities. Core sights include Stone Forest Scenic Area, Yunnan Provincial Museum, Dianchi Lake and Western Hills Scenic Area. They cover history, nature and city life, and can be combined freely to suit your interests. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or natural areas as time permits.

Food Culture: Yunnan's food is rich: crossing-the-bridge rice noodles have a savoury broth, steam-pot chicken is clear and fresh, flower cakes are delicately sweet with floral fragrance, and the wild mushrooms of the rainy season are what gourmets seek out — snacks are found on every street.

Stone Forest Scenic Area

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Stone Forest Scenic Area lies in Shilin Yi Autonomous County about 78 km southeast of Kunming. In 2007 it was inscribed as a World Natural Heritage site as part of the first phase of 'South China Karst', and it is a national 5A-level tourist attraction. The limestone here formed about 270 million years ago in the Permian period, and long dissolution, weathering and faulting have packed sword-, pillar- and tower-shaped stone peaks into a vast karst landscape. The scenic area comprises the Major Stone Forest with its tall peaks, the quieter, smaller Minor Stone Forest and the more pristine Naigu Stone Forest; visitors follow paths through stone lanes, lakes and cliff inscriptions. The local Sani people are a branch of the Yi, and both the Torch Festival and the Ashima legend originate here; from the viewing platform you can look over the whole sea of stone. Highlights include the Ten-Thousand-Year Lingzhi. Tip: a guide can explain how the stone forest formed and its legends. Practical info: located at Shilin Avenue, Shilin Yi Autonomous County, Kunming, Yunnan; ticket ¥130; opening hours 07:30–18:00.

Yunnan Provincial Museum

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

The new building of Yunnan Provincial Museum stands on Guangfu Road in Guandu District and opened in 2015, with a floor area of more than 60,000 square metres. Its collection numbers more than 230,000 artefacts, and permanent exhibitions include 'Ancient Yunnan', 'Light of Civilisation', 'Hegemony in the South', 'Land of Fragrant Buddhism', 'Opening the Frontier' and 'A Century of Storms'; ancient Dian bronze wares, Nanzhao and Dali Buddhist sculptures, and ethnic minority costumes and crafts are the focus. The best entry point for understanding Yunnan's 'unity in diversity', it holds ancient Dian bronzes, Nanzhao and Dali Buddhist art, ethnic minority costumes and Nie Er-related collections; entry is free, it is on the same line as Guandu Ancient Town, and it makes a good indoor half-day on a rainy day. Highlights include Nanzhao and Dali Buddhist sculptures and the general history of Yunnan. Tip: enter after reserving and follow the timeline from earliest times to the modern era; it is on the same line as Guandu Ancient Town — allow at least 2–3 hours. Practical info: located at 6393 Guangfu Road, Guandu District, Kunming; free entry (reservation required); opening hours 09:00–17:00 (closed Mondays, last entry 16:00).

Dianchi Lake

★★★★☆Recommend 4.5 / 5

Dianchi Lake lies south of Kunming and is the largest freshwater lake in Yunnan, covering more than 300 square kilometres at an elevation of about 1,886 metres; it is known as the 'pearl of the plateau'. Sun Ranweng's long couplet at Daguan Tower in the Qing dynasty opens with 'five hundred li of Dianchi rush before my eyes', capturing the beauty of lake and hills. The lake is divided into Caohai and Waihai; at the northern end, Haigeng Park and Haigeng Dam on the Caohai shore, together with Dragon Gate in the Western Hills and the western shore, form Kunming's classic lakeside route. Each year from November to March, tens of thousands of black-headed gulls migrate south from Siberia to winter here, with the peak from December to February; Cuihu Park and Haigeng Dam are the two liveliest watching spots. Urban expansion and eutrophication once degraded the water quality, but years of sewage interception, water diversion and wetland restoration have brought waterbirds and reeds back to the lakeside wetlands. Highlights include the sunset over Dianchi. Tip: winter is the best season for gull-watching, and you can buy bread to feed them. Practical info: located at Dianchi Road, Xishan District, Kunming, Yunnan; free entry; open all day.

Tip: Winter is the best season for gull-watching, and you can buy bread to feed them.

  • Address: Dianchi Road, Xishan District, Kunming, Yunnan

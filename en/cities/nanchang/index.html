City Intro

History & Culture: Nanchang was known in ancient times as Yuzhang and Hongdu, and takes its name from "prospering the southern frontier." On 1 August 1927 the Nanchang Uprising fired the first shot of armed resistance here, earning the city the name "the place where the military flag was raised." Its history and culture run deep: Wang Bo's "Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng" is celebrated through the ages, and the startling excavation of the Marquis of Haihun's tomb revealed Western Han civilization. Nanchang is where the August First military flag rose, and red culture is deeply rooted. The International Military Tattoo is held here on Army Day, with military bands from many countries performing together. Ganju is Jiangxi's local opera, with high and stirring arias. Tengwang Pavilion culture carries the timeless poetry of "sunset and a lone duck fly together, autumn water merges with the boundless sky." Nanchang people love mixed rice noodles and clay-pot soup; "a bowl of noodles and a bowl of soup in the morning" is the standard. Night tours of Tengwang Pavilion and the Gan River light show have become Nanchang's new calling cards, where tradition and modernity meet.

Geography: Located in northern Jiangxi, on the lower reaches of the Gan River and in the heart of the Poyang Lake Plain, Nanchang is embraced by river and lakes. The Gan River runs through the city, Poyang Lake — China's largest freshwater lake — lies to the north, and Tengwang Pavilion stands by the river. Meiling National Forest Park, with its overlapping green peaks, is a suburban summer retreat. Nanchang is in Jiangxi, where landforms, rivers and climate shape the city's spatial pattern. Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are pleasant; summer is hot but the Gan River night scenery is enchanting. Natural and cultural sites are fairly concentrated, making one- or two-day itineraries by area practical. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and scenic-area notices before departure.

Highlights: Climb Tengwang Pavilion to enjoy "sunset and a lone duck flying together," visit the August 1st Uprising Memorial Hall to recall red history, explore the Marquis of Haihun's tomb for Western Han treasures, and wander Meiling to breathe the natural oxygen bar. Core sights include Tengwang Pavilion, the August 1st Uprising Memorial Hall, Jiangxi Provincial Museum and the Haihunhou Kingdom Archaeological Site Park. They cover history, nature and city life and can be combined freely. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or nature as time allows.

Food Culture: Nanchang mixed rice noodles are smooth and spicy, clay-pot soup is clear and full-flavored, stir-fried cudweed with cured meat has a distinctive fragrance, and Gan River fish is tender and delicious — all showing the "fresh and spicy" character of Jiangxi cuisine.

Tengwang Pavilion

★★★★★Recommend 4.6 / 5

Tengwang Pavilion is one of the three great towers of Jiangnan, first built in the fourth year of the Tang Yonghui era (653) and made famous through the ages by Wang Bo's "Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng." The pavilion is 57.5 meters tall with nine storeys, and from the top visitors can look over the Gan River and Nanchang. Highlights include the main pavilion, the Gan River scenery, the "Seeking Dreams at Tengwang Pavilion" night performance and Wang Bo's poetry inscriptions. One of the three great towers of Jiangnan, it stands beside the Gan River and was made famous by Wang Bo's preface; climbing it gives views of the river and sky of "sunset and a lone duck flying together," and the night performance is staged here. Tip: go in the evening to see both sunset and the night view. Practical info: located at No. 58 Fanggu Street, Donghu District, Nanchang, Jiangxi; ticket ¥50; opening hours 08:00–18:30. Best season: spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are pleasant; summer is hot but the Gan River night scenery is enchanting. Combine with the August 1st Uprising Memorial Hall, Jiangxi Provincial Museum and Haihunhou Kingdom Archaeological Site Park for a half- or full-day itinerary.

Tip: Go in the evening to see both sunset and the night view.

  • Address: No. 58 Fanggu Street, Donghu District, Nanchang, Jiangxi
  • Ticket: ¥50
  • Opening: 08:00–18:30

August 1st Uprising Memorial Hall

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

The August 1st Uprising Memorial Hall stands at the former general headquarters of the Nanchang Uprising. Its displays of historical relics and rare photographs recreate the heroic course of the uprising on 1 August 1927 and make it an important red-tourism site. Highlights include the historical artifact displays, revolutionary history education and red culture experiences. Built around the former headquarters, it recreates the birth of the people's army and is a sacred site for red tourism and patriotic education. Tip: admission is free, and it is closed on Mondays. Practical info: located at No. 380 Zhongshan Road, Xihu District, Nanchang, Jiangxi; admission free; opening hours 09:00–17:00 (closed Mondays). Best season: spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are pleasant; summer is hot but the Gan River night scenery is enchanting. Combine with Tengwang Pavilion, Jiangxi Provincial Museum and the Haihunhou Kingdom Archaeological Site Park for a half- or full-day itinerary. Confirm the day's opening and reservation policy before setting out and allow enough time.

Tip: Admission is free, and it is closed on Mondays.

  • Address: No. 380 Zhongshan Road, Xihu District, Nanchang, Jiangxi
  • Ticket: Free
  • Opening: 09:00–17:00 (closed Mondays)

Jiangxi Provincial Museum

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

The new Jiangxi Provincial Museum stands on the north bank of the Gan River in Honggutan and opened in 2020, with a floor area of 86,000 square meters. Its collection is led by objects from the tomb of Liu He, Marquis of Haihun, including gold cakes, hoof-shaped gold, bronzes, lacquerware and bamboo slips; Shang bronzes from Xin'gan Dayangzhou, Jingdezhen ceramics and Jiangxi's revolutionary relics are also permanent strengths. Highlights include the gold from the Marquis of Haihun's tomb and Jiangxi's red culture. The provincial museum by the Gan River has the Haihun gold, bronzes and bamboo slips as its main attraction, with strong Shang bronzes and ceramics displays. Free with reservation, it shares the riverfront with Tengwang Pavilion and the August 1st Uprising Memorial Hall. Tip: enter after reserving, see the Haihun exhibition first, then the bronzes and ceramics; Tengwang Pavilion is across the river, so the two can fill half a day. Practical info: located at 698 Ganjiang North Avenue, Honggutan District, Nanchang; admission free (reservation required); opening hours 09:00–17:00 (closed Mondays, last entry 16:00).

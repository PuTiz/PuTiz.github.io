City Intro

History & Culture: Jiuquan is a key town at the western end of the Hexi Corridor. It was established as a commandery in the Han dynasty, and the Mogao Caves, Yumen Pass and Yangguan together formed the throat of the Silk Road. Jiuquan is home to the Dunhuang art tradition: the Mogao murals and painted sculptures span the Sixteen Kingdoms to the Yuan, while flying apsaras, sutra illustrations and the Library Cave manuscripts together tell the history of Silk Road civilisation. The city lies in Gansu and has developed a distinct urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls record different historical layers. Museums, historic districts and local festivals still make that history legible. History is written not only in sites and museums but also in dialect, food, festivals and everyday life.

Geography: The Qilian Mountains rise to the south and the Gobi stretches north, with oasis and desert alternating; the Dang River oasis at Dunhuang is the corridor's westernmost lifeline. Jiuquan lies in Gansu, where landforms, rivers and climate shape the city. Best May–October; September–October is cool with the best colours on the poplars and Gobi; in summer watch sun exposure and the day-night temperature difference. Natural and cultural sites are relatively concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and official notices. Roads and railways are the main connections, and major sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Highlights: See the Mogao murals, climb the Mingsha dunes to Crescent Lake, and pass through Yumen Gate for the desolate frontier of 'west of Yangguan no old friends remain'. Core sights include the Mogao Caves, Mingsha Mountain and Crescent Lake, Yumen Pass and Dunhuang Yardang National Geopark. They cover history, nature and city life, and can be combined freely. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and meals. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or nature as time allows. Local food is part of the itinerary — save a meal for a time-honoured shop or a local market.

Food Culture: Dunhuang donkey-meat noodles, lamb stewed with flatbread and apricot juice are representative; wheat noodles and lamb are the energy source of a Gobi journey.

Mogao Caves

★★★★★Recommend 4.9 / 5

The Mogao Caves were begun in 366 AD, the second year of the Jianyuan era of the Former Qin, and were built over the Sixteen Kingdoms, Northern Dynasties, Sui, Tang, Five Dynasties, Western Xia and Yuan. Today 735 caves survive, with 45,000 square metres of murals and more than 2,000 painted sculptures — the world's largest and richest surviving Buddhist art site. Cave 96's nine-storey facade, Cave 17 (the Library Cave), the Western Wei murals of Cave 285 and the early Tang music-and-dance scene of Cave 220 are must-sees. Visits begin with a digital film, then a shuttle bus to the caves, where a guide leads groups of eight caves. A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of China's four great grottoes, with 735 caves preserving 45,000 square metres of murals and more than 2,000 painted sculptures — a Buddhist art treasury on the Silk Road. Highlights include the Nine-storey Facade, Library Cave, Cave 285 and Cave 220. Tip: Book Type A tickets on the official mini-program in advance; tickets are tight in peak season; photography is forbidden inside; follow the guide and do not touch the murals. Practical info: located at Mogaozhen, Dunhuang, Jiuquan, Gansu; ticket peak ¥238 (Type A, includes digital show + 8 caves, reservation required); opening hours 08:00–18:00 (timed entry, book ahead in peak season).

  • Address: Mogaozhen, Dunhuang, Jiuquan, Gansu
  • Ticket: Peak ¥238 (Type A: digital show + 8 caves; reservation required)
  • Opening: 08:00–18:00 (timed entry; book ahead in peak season)

Mingsha Mountain and Crescent Lake

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

Mingsha Mountain and Crescent Lake lie 5 km south of Dunhuang. The dunes run about 40 km east-west and 20 km north-south, with the main peak at 1,715 metres. Crescent Lake is ringed by dunes, shaped like a new moon, covering about 13 mu, and has never been buried by sand. Visitors can ride camels, sand-sled or take off-road vehicles; climbing the dunes at dusk for sunset and the illuminated lake is the classic experience. Sand dunes and a crescent-shaped spring have coexisted south of Dunhuang for a thousand years; the sand never fills the spring and the spring never dries — the classic sunset and camel-riding spot. Highlights include Mingsha sunset and the camel caravan. Tip: enter after 5 pm, ride a camel first then climb for sunset; the sand is hot — wear sandals or rent shoe covers, and bring water and sun protection. Practical info: located at Mingsha Mountain and Crescent Lake Scenic Area, Dunhuang, Gansu; ticket ¥110 (camel, sand-sledding and other activities extra); opening hours 05:00–21:00 (longer in summer, best before sunset). Best season: May–October; September–October is cool with the best colours on the poplars and Gobi; in summer watch sun exposure and the day-night temperature difference.

Yumen Pass

★★★★☆Recommend 4.6 / 5

Yumen Pass was built under Emperor Wu of Han as a key gateway to the Western Regions, named because Hetian jade entered China through it. The surviving Xiaofangpan fort is a rammed-earth square about 10 metres high, part of the Han Great Wall system. Nearby are the Hecang granary, Han wall remains and the Yardang landforms, usually visited with the Yardang Geopark in one day. The first pass on the Silk Road after Dunhuang in the Han dynasty; the Xiaofangpan fort survives. Wang Zhihuan's line 'the spring wind never crosses Yumen Pass' made it a symbol of frontier poetry. Highlights include the Xiaofangpan fort, Han Great Wall and Hecang granary. Tip: hire a car with the Yardang trip; there are no services in the desert; it is windy and exposed — bring a jacket and water, and do not climb the rammed-earth walls. Practical info: located about 90 km northwest of Dunhuang, Gansu; ticket ¥40 (includes shuttle; subject to official notice); opening hours 08:00–18:00. Best season: May–October; September–October is cool with the best colours on the poplars and Gobi; in summer watch sun exposure and the day-night temperature difference.

City Intro

History & Culture: Altay Prefecture is in Xinjiang and is known for destinations such as Kanas Lake. Altay lies on the southern slopes of the Altai Mountains and has long been a corridor for pastoral migration and border trade. Altay Prefecture lies in Xinjiang and has formed a distinctive urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls record historical layers from different periods. Today museums, historic districts and local festivals still make the city's history legible. The city's history is written not only in sites and museums but also in dialect, food, festivals and the details of daily life. In modern times, transport building, industrial development and urban renewal have added further layers.

Geography: Altay Prefecture's core landscapes include Kanas Lake, Hemu Village, Baihaba and the Fish-Viewing Terrace. Altay Prefecture lies in Xinjiang, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's spatial pattern. June–September is best for grassland and lakes; mid-September to early October brings Kanas autumn colour. Winter brings snow scenery but access is limited. Natural and cultural sites are relatively concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary by area works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change the look of the landscape with the seasons; check the weather and official notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and the main sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Highlights: In Altay Prefecture, focus on Kanas Lake, Hemu Village and Baihaba. Core sights include Kanas Lake, Hemu Village, Baihaba and the Fish-Viewing Terrace. First-time visitors can start with the most representative core sights, then add museums, old streets or natural scenery as time allows. Kanas Lake

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

A glacial lake deep in the Altai Mountains whose water changes colour with the seasons, surrounded by Tuvan villages and birch forest. Highlights include Kanas Lake, the Fish-Viewing Terrace and Tuvan village. Tip: The scenic area is large — stay one night to see the morning mist; the autumn colour is best at the end of September. Practical info: located at Kanas, Burqin County, Altay Prefecture, Xinjiang; ticket ¥160 (sightseeing bus extra); opening hours 08:00–18:00. Best season: June–September for grassland and lakes; mid-September to early October for Kanas autumn colour. Winter brings snow scenery but access is limited. It can be combined with Hemu Village, Baihaba and the Fish-Viewing Terrace in Altay Prefecture for a half-day or one-day itinerary. Before setting out, confirm the day's opening and reservation policies and allow plenty of time for the visit. In peak season or on holidays, book ahead and travel outside peak times, and allow time for queues and security checks. Plan your length of stay around the opening hours and your energy. If carrying luggage or travelling with children, allow more time for security checks and walking.

  • Address: Kanas, Burqin County, Altay Prefecture, Xinjiang
  • Ticket: ¥160 (sightseeing bus extra)
  • Opening: 08:00–18:00

Hemu Village

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

A Tuvan village where log houses, cooking smoke and birch forest look like a fairy tale in the morning mist — Kanas's most popular sunrise spot. Highlights include the Hemu viewing platform and the log-house village. Tip: Stay in the village to see the sunrise and go up to the viewing platform early; in autumn rooms are hard to find. Culture

Altay is home to Kazakh and Tuvan communities. Kazakh dombra songs, horse racing, diaoyang (goat-grabbing) and the Sawuerdeng dance, together with Tuvan khoomei and choor flute playing, shape the pastoral culture of the Altai Mountains.

City Intro

History & Culture: Weinan lies in eastern Guanzhong and is one of the important birthplaces of Chinese civilisation; Huashan, the Xiyue Temple, Sima Qian's shrine and Tongguan together form a cultural corridor in eastern Guanzhong. Huashan is the fourth Daoist grotto-heaven, and the Xiyue Temple was where emperors of successive dynasties sacrificed to the Western Peak; Weinan is also the hometown of Sima Qian and Yang Hucheng, where Guanzhong folk customs and Huashan Daoist culture intertwine. Weinan is located in Shaanxi Province and has formed a distinctive urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls from different periods remain as historical layers. Today museums, historic districts and local festivals still make the city's historical thread legible.

Geography: The Qinling Mountains rise to the south and the Wei River runs to the north; Huashan's granite peak forest rises abruptly at the eastern end of the Guanzhong plain, and the Wei River alluvial plain is one of Shaanxi's granaries. Weinan is located in Shaanxi Province, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's spatial pattern. Best April–June and September–October; Huashan has frequent summer thunderstorms and snow and ice in winter, so check the weather before climbing. Natural and cultural sights are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary can be arranged by area. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and official scenic-area notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and the main sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Highlights: Climb Huashan at night to see the sunrise, visit the Xiyue Temple, walk the Xianyu gorge, then go to Tongguan to see the Yellow River and the old city. Core sights include Huashan, Xiyue Temple, Xianyu Gorge and Yuquan Temple. These sights cover different sides of history, nature and city life and can be combined freely according to interest. It is worth linking the core sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half-day or full-day itinerary. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest for a fuller experience. First-time visitors can start with the most representative core sights, then add museums, old streets or natural areas as time allows. Local food is part of the itinerary too — save mealtime for a time-honoured shop or a local market.

Food Culture: Huayin xue noodles, dadao noodles, Tongguan roujiamo and Huashan vegetarian dishes give equal weight to wheat foods and mountain flavours.

Huashan

★★★★★Recommend 4.9 / 5

Huashan lies south of Huayin city; the main peak, South Peak, is 2,154.9 metres above sea level and is the highest of the Five Great Mountains. Huashan is known for its granite fault-block mountain and perilous paths; the East, West, South, North and Central peaks stand in a ring, and the Plank Road in the Sky, Sparrowhawk Turn, Thousand-Foot Precipice and Hundred-Foot Gorge are classic perilous routes. The classic route is up by the West Peak cable car, then South Peak, East Peak, Central Peak and North Peak, or a night climb from Yuquan Temple to see sunrise on East Peak. The Western Peak of the Five Great Mountains, famed as “the most precipitous mountain under heaven”, with the Plank Road in the Sky, the Sparrowhawk Turn, East Peak sunrise and the West Peak cable car forming the classic climbing route. Tip: The night climb from Yuquan Temple takes about 6–8 hours — bring a torch and warm clothing; the Plank Road in the Sky and Sparrowhawk Turn require a separately purchased safety harness, and those with a fear of heights should not attempt them. Practical info: located at Huashan Town, Huayin, Weinan, Shaanxi; ticket peak season ¥160 (cable car and mountain shuttle extra); opening hours open all day (cable cars about 07:00–19:00; night climbing requires booking). Best season: April–June and September–October; Huashan has frequent summer thunderstorms and snow and ice in winter, so check the weather before climbing.

  • Address: Huashan Town, Huayin, Weinan, Shaanxi
  • Ticket: Peak season ¥160 (cable car and mountain shuttle extra)
  • Opening: Open all day (cable cars about 07:00–19:00; night climbing requires booking)

Xiyue Temple

★★★★☆Recommend 4.6 / 5

Xiyue Temple is in the urban area of Huayin and was first built during the Yuanguang era of Emperor Wu of Han; it was the place where emperors of successive dynasties sacrificed to the Great Emperor of the Western Peak, Huashan. The temple faces south, and along its central axis are the Haoling Gate, Five Phoenix Tower, Lingxing Gate, Jincheng Gate, Haoling Hall, the sleeping palace and the Imperial Library; its layout imitates the Forbidden City in Beijing, hence the name “Forbidden City of Shaanxi”. The temple has many steles and ancient cypresses and is the most complete group of ancient buildings at the foot of Huashan. The imperial temple where emperors of successive dynasties sacrificed to Huashan, founded under Emperor Wu of Han; the surviving buildings follow the Ming-Qing layout and it is known as the “Forbidden City of Shaanxi”. Highlights include the ancient cypresses and steles. Tip: Arrange it on the same day as Huashan and see the Xiyue Temple before climbing; the architectural axis rewards a slow walk, and a guide is recommended. Practical info: located at Yuemiao Street, Huayin, Weinan, Shaanxi; ticket ¥30 (subject to official notice); opening hours 08:00–18:00. Best season: April–June and September–October; Huashan has frequent summer thunderstorms and snow and ice in winter, so check the weather before climbing.

  • Address: Yuemiao Street, Huayin, Weinan, Shaanxi
  • Ticket: ¥30 (subject to official notice)
  • Opening: 08:00–18:00

Xianyu Gorge

★★★★☆Recommend 4.5 / 5

Xianyu lies on the west side of Huashan's main peak and is a hiking route dominated by streams, pools and waterfalls and a granite gorge. Water volume in the valley changes with the seasons — cool in summer and red leaves in autumn; the trail follows the stream with gentle gradients and suits visitors who do not want to climb Huashan's main peak or who are travelling with elderly people or children. It can be arranged on the same day as Huashan and the Xiyue Temple. A canyon scenic area at the foot of Huashan, where streams, pools and waterfalls link up with granite cliffs; easier than the main peak and good for hiking and escaping the heat. Highlights include the streams and pools, and easy hiking. Tip: Water is high in summer and the rocks are slippery; wear non-slip shoes and bring mosquito repellent. Practical info: located at Xianyu Scenic Area, Huayin, Weinan, Shaanxi; ticket about ¥30 (subject to official notice); opening hours 08:00–17:30. Best season: April–June and September–October; Huashan has frequent summer thunderstorms and snow and ice in winter, so check the weather before climbing. It can be combined with Huashan, Xiyue Temple and Yuquan Temple in Weinan for a half-day or full-day itinerary.

  • Address: Xianyu Scenic Area, Huayin, Weinan, Shaanxi
  • Ticket: About ¥30 (subject to official notice)
  • Opening: 08:00–17:30

City Intro

History & Culture: Zhangye takes its name from the phrase “spreading the empire's arm to reach the Western Regions” and was a key Silk Road town in the middle section of the Hexi Corridor; the Giant Buddha Temple, Mati Temple and the Heishuiguo ruins bear witness to the mixing of many ethnic groups. Zhangye is a meeting place of ethnic cultures in the Hexi Corridor, where Yugur, Tibetan and Han cultures coexist on the northern slope of the Qilian Mountains; the Mati Temple caves and the high-relief painted sculptures of Jinta Temple are treasures of early Buddhist art. Zhangye lies in Gansu Province and has formed a distinctive urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls from different periods remain as historical layers. Today museums, historic districts and local festivals still make the city's historical thread legible.

Geography: The Qilian Mountains rise to the south and the Heli Mountains stand to the north; the Hei River has built the Zhangye oasis, and Danxia, wetlands, grasslands and Gobi all lie within a day's drive. Zhangye is located in Gansu Province, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's spatial pattern. Best June–October; September–October has the strongest Danxia colours; summer brings a big day-night temperature difference, so take care with sun protection. Natural and cultural sights are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary can be arranged by area. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and official scenic-area notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and the main sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Highlights: See the sunset over Rainbow Danxia, visit the reclining Buddha at the Giant Buddha Temple, climb to the Mati Temple caves, then go to the wetland park to see reeds and migratory birds. Core sights include Zhangye Rainbow Danxia, Zhangye Giant Buddha Temple, Mati Temple and Pingshanhu Canyon. These sights cover different sides of history, nature and city life and can be combined freely according to interest. It is worth linking the core sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half-day or full-day itinerary. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest for a fuller experience. First-time visitors can start with the most representative core sights, then add museums, old streets or natural areas as time allows. Local food is part of the itinerary too — save mealtime for a time-honoured shop or a local market.

Food Culture: Cuoyu noodles, beef xiaofan, Linze jujubes and Shandan lamb are the Hexi flavours that blend oasis farming with nomadic tastes.

Zhangye Rainbow Danxia

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

Zhangye Rainbow Danxia lies in Nijiaying Town, Linze County, covers about 50 square kilometres and is known for the combined landscape of coloured hills and Danxia landforms. Under tectonic uplift and weathering, Cretaceous sandstone and conglomerate have formed bands of red, yellow, orange, white and grey-green; the colours are most saturated at sunset and after rain. The scenic area has four viewing platforms linked by shuttle, and Platform 4 is the best place to watch the sunset. One of China's most beautiful Danxia landscapes, where the coloured hills reveal layers of red, yellow, orange and white at sunset like a spilled palette. Highlights include Platform 4, Rainbow Screen and Great Scallop. Tip: Enter after 4 pm and take the shuttle to Platform 4 to wait for sunset; the colours are best after rain, but the boardwalks are slippery. Practical info: located at Nijiaying Town, Linze County, Zhangye, Gansu; ticket ¥74 (includes shuttle; subject to official notice); opening hours 06:00–19:30 (best before sunset). Best season: June–October; September–October has the strongest Danxia colours; summer brings a big day-night temperature difference, so take care with sun protection.

  • Address: Nijiaying Town, Linze County, Zhangye, Gansu
  • Ticket: ¥74 (includes shuttle; subject to official notice)
  • Opening: 06:00–19:30 (best before sunset)

Zhangye Giant Buddha Temple

★★★★☆Recommend 4.6 / 5

Zhangye Giant Buddha Temple was first built in the first year of the Yongan era of Emperor Chongzong of Western Xia (1098) and was an imperial temple of Western Xia, originally named Jiaye Rulai Temple. The reclining Buddha in the Great Buddha Hall is 34.5 metres long and 7.5 metres across the shoulders; it has a wooden core with clay modelling and is gilded and painted, and is the largest surviving indoor reclining Buddha in China. The temple also has an earthen pagoda, a sutra library and Ming-Qing Buddhist scriptures, and is an important relic for the study of Western Xia Buddhism and Hexi architecture. A Western Xia imperial temple whose 34.5-metre reclining Buddha is the largest surviving indoor clay reclining Buddha in China, with Ming-Qing scriptures preserved in the sutra library. Tip: Photography is not allowed in the Reclining Buddha Hall; the temple is within walking distance of other sights in central Zhangye, and a guide is recommended to explain the Western Xia history. Practical info: located at Dafosi Lane, Ganzhou District, Zhangye, Gansu; ticket ¥40 (subject to official notice); opening hours 08:00–18:00. Best season: June–October; September–October has the strongest Danxia colours; summer brings a big day-night temperature difference, so take care with sun protection. It can be combined with Zhangye Rainbow Danxia, Mati Temple and Pingshanhu Canyon in Zhangye for a half-day or full-day itinerary.

  • Address: Dafosi Lane, Ganzhou District, Zhangye, Gansu
  • Ticket: ¥40 (subject to official notice)
  • Opening: 08:00–18:00

Mati Temple

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Mati Temple is located in Mati Township, Sunan Yugur Autonomous County, was first built in the Northern Liang and takes its name from the legend that a heavenly horse left its hoofprint here. The core Thirty-Three Heavens caves were cut into a red sandstone cliff, with seven levels and twenty-one caves distributed vertically along the cliff face; the caves are linked by narrow walkways that require hands and feet to climb. Nearby are the Thousand Buddha Caves, Jinta Temple and the Yugur grasslands, where natural and cultural landscapes coexist. A cave complex on the northern slope of the Qilian Mountains, where the Thirty-Three Heavens caves are cut into a red sandstone cliff and walkways climb vertically — one of the most thrilling grottoes in the Hexi Corridor. Tip: The Thirty-Three Heavens caves require climbing narrow walkways, so those with a fear of heights or limited mobility should be cautious; hiring a car with Danxia on the same day involves many winding mountain roads. Practical info: located at Mati Tibetan Township, Sunan Yugur Autonomous County, Zhangye, Gansu; ticket about ¥74 (includes the Thirty-Three Heavens caves; subject to official notice); opening hours 08:00–18:00. Best season: June–October; September–October has the strongest Danxia colours; summer brings a big day-night temperature difference, so take care with sun protection.

  • Address: Mati Tibetan Township, Sunan Yugur Autonomous County, Zhangye, Gansu
  • Ticket: About ¥74 (includes the Thirty-Three Heavens caves; subject to official notice)
  • Opening: 08:00–18:00

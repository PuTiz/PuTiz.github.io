City Intro

History & Culture: Chengde lies in the heart of the Yan Mountains in northeastern Hebei, bordering Beijing and Tianjin to the south, a corridor linking the northeast with north China. In the Qing dynasty, the Kangxi and Qianlong emperors built the Mountain Resort here as a summer capital and second political centre, laying down the city's plan; its old Mongolian name was 'Haerha Hetun'. Chengde's cultural heritage is deep and varied. The Mountain Resort and the Outer Eight Temples testify to Qing imperial summer life and ethnic integration, while the Tibetan architecture of Putuozongcheng Temple and the gilded roof of Xumifushou Temple show the meeting of Han and Tibetan art. The annual Chengde International Tourism Festival, Mountain Resort Culture Festival and Imperial Temple Fair keep this 'Capital Beyond the Great Wall' culturally alive. Chengde sits in Hebei Province and has formed a distinct urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange.

Geography: Chengde lies in the Yan Mountains, with a cool, pleasant climate and no fierce summer heat — a natural summer retreat. Mountains, water and imperial grandeur blend here, earning it the name 'Capital Beyond the Great Wall'. Chengde sits in Hebei Province, where landforms, rivers and climate have together shaped the city's spatial pattern. Summer (Jun–Aug) is cool and pleasant, a summer retreat; autumn (Sep–Oct) turns the woods red and gold with delightful scenery. Natural and cultural sites are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary by area works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and official notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and the main sights can be linked from the city or county town.

Highlights: The Mountain Resort is a UNESCO World Heritage Site that combines Jiangnan gardens with the bold mountains of the north; the Outer Eight Temples blend the finest Han and Tibetan architecture; Putuozongcheng Temple copies the Potala Palace and is strikingly imposing; and Qingchui Peak stands alone as a strange rock pinnacle. Core sights include the Mountain Resort, Puning Temple, Putuozongcheng Temple and Xumifushou Temple. They cover history, nature and city life, and can be combined freely. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and meals. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or nature as time allows.

Food Culture: Chengde cuisine is represented by baked biscuits, lamb soup and lotus-leaf chicken — plain, honest flavours with both court and mountain-country character.

Mountain Resort

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Work began in 1703, the 42nd year of the Kangxi reign, and was not completed until 1792, the 57th year of Qianlong, taking 89 years across three reigns. The grounds cover 564 hectares, and the palace wall follows the slope for about ten kilometres — the largest surviving imperial garden in the world. Hills make up about four fifths of the estate, while the lake district holds eight lakes of various sizes; Kangxi and Qianlong each titled thirty-six views, together forming the seventy-two. The main hall, Danbo Jingcheng Hall, stands on 48 great nanmu columns. The Qing court received the returning Torghut leader Ubashi and the visiting Sixth Panchen Lama here. Listed as a national key cultural relic in 1961, the resort entered the World Heritage List in 1994 as the Mountain Resort and its Outlying Temples. Highlights include the Danbo Jingcheng Hall, Yanyu Tower, Rehe Spring and the Mongolian yurts. Tip: Allow half a day; electric carts run inside the scenic area. Summer is best for escaping the heat, and the autumn foliage should not be missed. Practical info: located at 6 Shanzhuang East Road, Shuangqiao District, Chengde, Hebei Province; admission ¥130 (peak season); open 07:00–18:00.

Tip: Allow half a day for the visit; electric carts are available within the scenic area.

Puning Temple

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Puning Temple was first built in 1755, the 20th year of the Qianlong reign, to commemorate the pacification of the Dzungars, its name meaning 'universal peace'. The front half follows the Han-style seven-hall monastery layout, while the rear centres on the Tibetan-style Mahayana Hall, forming a Han-Tibetan composition. The Mahayana Hall is 36.75 metres high and houses a wooden thousand-armed, thousand-eyed Guanyin 22.28 metres tall, carved from pine, cypress, elm, linden and fir — one of China's largest surviving wooden Guanyin statues. A Han-Tibetan temple built by Qianlong to commemorate the pacification of the Dzungars, whose Mahayana Hall houses a 22.28-metre thousand-armed Guanyin, one of China's largest wooden Guanyin statues. Compared with the other Outer Eight Temples, both its incense and the scale of its architecture are more striking. Highlights include the Han-Tibetan layout and the Qianlong stele. Tip: It is on the same route as the Mountain Resort, so visit Puning first in the morning; photography is forbidden inside the halls, and religious etiquette should be respected. Practical info: located at 1 Puning Road, Shuangqiao District, Chengde; admission ¥80 (peak season; subject to official notice); open 08:00–17:30.

Putuozongcheng Temple

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Construction began in 1767, the 32nd year of the Qianlong reign, and was completed in 1771, the 36th year, to mark the Qianlong Emperor's 60th birthday and the empress dowager's 80th. The temple covers 220,000 square metres and contains about 60 buildings, rising in stages from the mountain gate, stele pavilion and Five-Stupa Gate to the glazed archway. At the rear the Great Red Platform stands 43 metres high; its outer wall appears to have seven storeys but inside there are three tiers of galleries around the central Wanfa Guiyi Hall, whose roof is covered with gilded fish-scale copper tiles. In the Qing dynasty twelve Tibetan Buddhist temples were built around the resort, eight of them administered by officials of the Lifanyuan and commonly called the Outer Eight Temples; they joined the World Heritage List with the Mountain Resort in 1994. Known as the Little Potala Palace, it copies Tibet's Potala Palace, with red and white platforms rising in tiers, and is the largest temple among the Outer Eight Temples. Tip: Combine it with the Mountain Resort on a joint ticket to learn about Qing ethnic policy and religious culture. Practical info: located on Huancheng North Road, Shuangqiao District, Chengde, Hebei Province; admission ¥80; open 08:00–17:30.

Tip: Best combined with a Mountain Resort ticket to understand Qing ethnic policy and religious culture.

  • Address: Huancheng North Road, Shuangqiao District, Chengde, Hebei Province
  • Ticket: ¥80
  • Opening: 08:00–17:30

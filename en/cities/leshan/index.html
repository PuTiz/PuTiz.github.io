City Intro

History & Culture: Leshan, known in ancient times as Jiazhou, is where the Min, Qingyi and Dadu rivers meet; the Leshan Giant Buddha, carved in the Tang dynasty, and Emei Shan together form a World Heritage Site. The Leshan Giant Buddha was begun in the first year of the Kaiyuan era of the Tang (713) and took 90 years to complete; Emei Shan is Samantabhadra's sacred mountain, where Buddhist culture and mountain ecology coexist. Leshan lies in Sichuan and has developed a distinct urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls remain as historical layers from different periods. Museums, historic districts and local festivals still make the city's history legible today. History is written not only in sites and museums but also in dialect, food, festivals and the details of everyday life.

Geography: The meeting of the three rivers has carved the Lingyun Mountain cliff, where the Buddha sits against the rock; Emei Shan rises straight from the plain, its Golden Summit at 3,079 metres. Leshan lies in Sichuan, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's layout. Best March–June and September–November; Emei Shan is cool in summer, and the Golden Summit has snow in winter. Natural and cultural sites are relatively concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary can be arranged by area. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons, so check the weather and official notices before setting out. Roads and railways are the main connections, and the major sights can be linked from the city or the county town.

Highlights: See the Leshan Giant Buddha, climb to the Golden Summit of Emei Mountain, ride the Jiayang Steam Train, then spend a day eating snacks. Core sights include the Leshan Giant Buddha, Emei Mountain, Dongfang Fodu and the Jiayang Steam Train. These sights cover different sides of history, nature and city life, and can be combined freely according to interest. Pair the core sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day itinerary. If time allows, slow down and leave time for walking and rest for a fuller experience. First-time visitors can start with the most representative core sights, then add museums, old streets or natural scenery as time allows. Local food is part of the itinerary too; save mealtimes for time-honoured shops or local markets.

Food Culture: Bobo chicken, sweet-skin duck, qiaojiao beef and doufunao: Leshan is recognised as Sichuan's city of snacks.

Leshan Giant Buddha

★★★★★Recommend 4.9 / 5

The world's largest stone seated Maitreya, 71 metres tall, carved into the Lingyun Mountain cliff with the confluence of three rivers at its feet. Main highlights include the 71-metre Buddha, the Nine-Turn Cliff Walk and Lingyun Temple. Visiting tip: in peak season the queue down the Nine-Turn Cliff Walk is very long; a sightseeing boat gives the full panorama, and mornings are quieter. Practical info: the site is at Lingyun Road, Shizhong District, Leshan, Sichuan; ticket ¥80 (boat extra); opening hours 07:30–18:30. Best season: March–June and September–November; Emei Shan is cool in summer, and the Golden Summit has snow in winter. It can be combined with Emei Mountain, Dongfang Fodu and the Jiayang Steam Train in Leshan for a half-day or full-day itinerary. Before setting out, check the day's opening and reservation policy and allow plenty of time. In peak season or on holidays, book ahead and travel off-peak, and allow time for queues and security checks. Plan your stay around the opening hours and your energy. If you are carrying luggage or travelling with children, allow more time for security checks and walking.

Emei Mountain

★★★★★Recommend 4.9 / 5

One of China's four great Buddhist mountains and Samantabhadra's sacred site; the Golden Summit sunrise, sea of clouds, Buddha's halo and the Ten-Direction Samantabhadra statue make up the classic Emei impression. Main highlights include Qingyin Pavilion and the monkey zone. Visiting tip: the Golden Summit requires the shuttle bus plus cable car, and it is cold on the mountain, so bring a jacket; monkeys snatch food, so do not carry plastic bags in your hands. Practical info: the site is at Emeishan, Leshan, Sichuan; ticket ¥160 (shuttle bus and cable car extra); opening hours 06:00–18:00 (Golden Summit open all year). Best season: March–June and September–November; Emei Shan is cool in summer, and the Golden Summit has snow in winter. It can be combined with the Leshan Giant Buddha, Dongfang Fodu and the Jiayang Steam Train in Leshan for a half-day or full-day itinerary. Dongfang Fodu

★★★★☆Recommend 4.4 / 5

A Buddhist art theme park beside the Leshan Giant Buddha, with replicas and re-creations of famous Buddhist statues from China and abroad; easy to visit on the same day as the Giant Buddha. Main highlights include the groups of Buddhist statues, the underground palace, stone-carving art and Lingyun Mountain. Visiting tip: it is on the same route as the Leshan Giant Buddha, so if time is short you can see only the Giant Buddha; the park has many steps. Practical info: the site is at Lingyun Road, Shizhong District, Leshan, Sichuan; ticket ¥80 (subject to official notice); opening hours 08:00–18:00. Local Food

Bobo chicken
¥20–35/person
Cold skewers soaked in red chilli oil or green Sichuan pepper broth, with chicken, lotus root and wood ear chosen to taste — numbing, spicy, fresh and fragrant.
★ 4.8
Sweet-skin duck
¥30–50/person
A famous Leshan dish: the duck skin is brushed with maltose and fried until crisp and sweet, while the meat stays tender and savoury; good served cold or hot.
★ 4.7
Qiaojiao beef
¥25–40/person
Beef and beef offal blanched in clear broth and dipped in ground dried chilli; the soup is fresh and the meat tender. A Leshan breakfast and late-night snack.

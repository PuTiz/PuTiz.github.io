City Intro

History & Culture: Hulunbuir lies in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region and is the gateway to Hulunbuir Grassland and other destinations, and was historically a regional transport and trading node. Hulunbuir was historically pasture and hunting land for Mongol, Ewenki and other peoples; the Qing established the Hulunbuir Vice-Dutong, and the Chinese Eastern Railway later spurred border trade. Hulunbuir lies in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region and has formed a distinct urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, walls, lanes, temples and guild halls remain as historical layers from different periods. Museums, historic districts and local festivals still make the city's history legible today. History is written not only in sites and museums but also in dialect, food, festivals and the details of everyday life.

Geography: Hulunbuir's core landscapes include Hulunbuir Grassland, Moriger River, Erguna Wetland and Manzhouli Matryoshka Square, with natural and cultural sights existing side by side within short distances. Hulunbuir lies in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's layout. Hulunbuir Grassland

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

One of the world's four great grasslands; in summer green waves stretch to the horizon with herds of cattle and sheep, making it the first choice for horse riding, staying in a yurt and stargazing. Main highlights include the Moriger River and the grassland night sky. Visiting tip: the temperature difference between day and night on the grassland is large, so bring a jacket; choose a licensed horse ranch for riding and do not go far from the road alone. Practical info: the site is at Chen Barag Banner, Hulunbuir, Inner Mongolia; ticket free (grassland scenic-area activities extra); opening hours all day. Best season: June–September when the grassland is at its loveliest, with lush grass and water in July–August; winter is cold but offers snowfields and Nadam fairs. It can be combined with the Moriger River, Erguna Wetland and Manzhouli Matryoshka Square in Hulunbuir for a half-day or full-day itinerary. Moriger River

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Known as “the finest winding river under heaven”, its channel loops again and again across the grassland; best at sunset for aerial photography and for watching herds return to their pens. Main highlights include the river's nine bends, aerial photography over the grassland and the herds. Visiting tip: the viewing platform is windy, so arrive an hour before sunset; the grassland has no shade, so take care with sun protection. Practical info: the site is at Chen Barag Banner, Hulunbuir, Inner Mongolia; ticket free (viewing platform and activities extra); opening hours all day. Erguna Wetland

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

One of Asia's largest woody wetlands, where the Gen River winds between grassland and birch forest; in autumn the woods are ablaze with colour, and the viewing platform gives a panorama of the wetland. Main highlights include the Gen River's winding course and the autumn colours of the forest. Visiting tip: it is most beautiful in mid-to-late September; the viewing platform has many steps, so wear comfortable shoes. Practical info: the site is at Ergun, Hulunbuir, Inner Mongolia; ticket ¥65 (includes shuttle bus; subject to official notice); opening hours 07:30–18:00. Culture

Hulunbuir is home to Mongol, Ewenki, Russian and other communities. Naadam, long song, khoomei and morin khuur, the Russian Basky festival and the reindeer culture of the Aoluguya Ewenki together shape the grassland and forest way of life.

City Intro

History & Culture: Yangzhou lies in central Jiangsu where the Yangtze River meets the Beijing–Hangzhou Grand Canal, and is among the first group of national historical and cultural cities designated by the State Council. Founded more than 2,500 years ago, it was the great southeastern metropolis of the Sui and Tang and a salt-merchant stronghold in the Ming and Qing; 'to Yangzhou in the misty third month' has been celebrated through the ages. Yangzhou is the birthplace of Huaiyang cuisine, with a refined and particular food culture. Yangzhou lacquerware decoration and woodblock printing are national-level intangible cultural heritage. The Slender West Lake Tourism Festival and the Misty Third Month Tourism Festival draw visitors from far and wide. Yangzhou's garden and salt-merchant culture and its canal culture run deep, and poets and writers have left countless verses. Yangzhou lies in Jiangsu Province and has formed a distinctive urban memory through regional history and cultural exchange. From ancient settlements to the modern city, city walls, lanes, temples and guild halls record historical layers from different periods.

Geography: The land is flat with a dense network of waterways; the Shugang–Slender West Lake Scenic Area has shimmering water and elegant gardens. The climate is subtropical monsoon, warm and humid with four distinct seasons. Yangzhou is located in Jiangsu Province, where landforms, rivers and climate together shape the city's layout. Spring (Mar–May), the 'misty third month', brings peach and willow along the banks; autumn (Sep–Nov) is clear and brisk. Natural and cultural sites are relatively concentrated, so one- or two-day itineraries by area work well. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons, so check the weather and scenic-area notices before setting out. Road and rail are the main connections, and major sights can be linked from the city or county seat.

Highlights: The Five-Pavilion Bridge on Slender West Lake, the four-season rockeries of Ge Garden and the bustle of Dongguan Street; experience the easy life of morning tea and an evening bath. Core sights include Slender West Lake, Ge Garden, He Garden and Dongguan Street. They cover history, nature and city life and can be combined freely. Pair the core sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or natural areas as time permits. Local food is part of the itinerary — save a meal for a time-honoured shop or a local market.

Food Culture: Yangzhou is the birthplace of Huaiyang cuisine, one of China's four great cuisines. The fried rice has separate, distinct grains; crab-roe soup dumplings have thin skins and plentiful broth; lion's-head meatballs are tender and not greasy; and the morning-tea culture is refined and particular.

Slender West Lake

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Slender West Lake is Yangzhou's most famous sight. The lake is long and narrow, winding and restrained, known for its delicate grace. The Five-Pavilion Bridge is its landmark, and the Twenty-Four Bridge is famous from Du Mu's poem. Highlights include the White Pagoda and Xiaojinshan. Tip: go in spring, when the peach blossom and willows are at their loveliest. Practical info: located at 28 Dahongqiao Road, Hanjiang District, Yangzhou, Jiangsu Province; ticket ¥100 (peak season); opening hours 06:30–18:00. Best season: spring (Mar–May), the 'misty third month', when peach and willow line the banks; autumn (Sep–Nov) is clear and brisk. Combine with Ge Garden, He Garden and Dongguan Street for a half- or full-day itinerary. Check the day's opening and booking policy before you go, and allow enough time. In peak season or on holidays, book ahead and avoid the crowds, allowing time for queues and security checks.

Ge Garden

★★★★★Recommend 4.6 / 5

Ge Garden was the private garden of the Qing salt merchant Huang Zhiyun. Its name comes from the shape of a bamboo leaf, which resembles the character ge ('individual'); bamboo is planted widely and the garden is celebrated for its bamboo and stones. Bamboo-shoot stones, lake stones, yellow stones and Xuan stones are piled into rockeries for spring, summer, autumn and winter, set out in a sequence both circular and varied — a layout famous for its four-season rockeries and unique in Chinese garden design, standing alongside He Garden, known as the 'first garden of the late Qing', in the old city. Highlights include the spring rockery (bamboo-shoot stone), summer rockery (lake stone), autumn rockery (yellow stone) and winter rockery (Xuan stone). Tip: hire a guide to explain the design ideas behind the four-season rockeries for a fuller appreciation of the garden art. Practical info: located at 10 Yanfu East Road, Guangling District, Yangzhou, Jiangsu Province; ticket ¥45; opening hours 07:30–17:30. Best season: spring (Mar–May), the 'misty third month', when peach and willow line the banks; autumn (Sep–Nov) is clear and brisk.

He Garden

★★★★☆Recommend 4.5 / 5

He Garden is a masterpiece of late Qing garden design, built by He Zhidao. A two-level covered walkway runs through the whole garden; the Pian Stone Mountain House preserves the rockery work of the master Shi Tao, and Chinese and Western architectural styles are combined. Highlights include the Qima Tower and Yuxiu Tower. Tip: He Garden is called the first garden of the late Qing; visit it the same day as Ge Garden for comparison. Practical info: located at 66 Xuningmen Avenue, Guangling District, Yangzhou, Jiangsu Province; ticket ¥45; opening hours 07:30–17:30. Best season: spring (Mar–May), the 'misty third month', when peach and willow line the banks; autumn (Sep–Nov) is clear and brisk.

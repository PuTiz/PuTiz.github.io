City Intro

History & Culture: Shanghai grew from a fishing village into the greatest metropolis of modern East Asia. The 52 buildings of varied style along the Bund and the shikumen lane houses bear witness to more than a century of East–West exchange since the port opened, making the city both a microcosm of modern Chinese history and a contemporary international financial centre. Haipai culture is Shanghai's soul, its core the idea of embracing all rivers and accommodating every influence. Shikumen architecture is a typical East–West hybrid, and in the lanes old Shanghai's everyday life coexists with cafés and galleries. Huju opera tells everyday joys and sorrows in the soft Wu dialect, while pingtan storytelling, with pipa and sanxian, speaks of Jiangnan refinement. The Bund's foreign buildings face Pudong's skyscrapers across the river, symbolising a dialogue between tradition and modernity. From the qipao fashion of the 1920s and 1930s to today's international arts festivals and fashion weeks, Shanghai has always been China's window on the world, showing a distinctive charm born of East and West.

Geography: Shanghai stands on the south bank of the Yangtze estuary. The Huangpu River runs through the city, dividing it into Pudong and Puxi. The land is flat with a dense network of waterways, and the climate is subtropical monsoon, with four distinct seasons and ample rain. As a municipality directly under the central government, Shanghai's urban space, rivers and climate have shaped one another into a compact, multi-centred city. Spring (March–May) and autumn (October–November) are mild, with shady plane trees — the best time for strolling the Bund, Yuyuan and Tianzifang. Natural and cultural sights are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary by area works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and official notices before setting out.

Highlights: See the illuminated foreign architecture along the Bund, ride up the Oriental Pearl Tower for views over the Huangpu, wander the old town around Yuyuan and cruise the river at night, taking in the weave of modern metropolis and nostalgia. Core sights include the Bund, Shanghai Museum, Oriental Pearl Radio and TV Tower and Yuyuan Garden. They cover history, nature and city life and can be combined freely. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and rest. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or natural areas as time allows.

Food Culture: Shanghainese cooking is rich in oil and soy sauce, savoury with a hint of sweetness: shengjian buns have crisp bottoms and abundant juice, xiaolongbao have thin skins and savoury soup, and pork chop with rice cake is crisp outside and glutinous within. Haipai snacks absorb flavours from everywhere yet have a character of their own.

The Bund

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

The Bund runs from Waibaidu Bridge in the north to Jinling East Road in the south, stretching about 1.5 kilometres along the river. Most of the 52 buildings on its western side were built between 1920 and 1930, and Neoclassical, Gothic Revival, Baroque and Art Deco façades stand side by side, forming the Gallery of World Architecture. The Customs House, completed in December 1927 and designed by the British firm Palmer & Turner, is about 79 metres tall; its great clock follows the pattern of London's Big Ben and has rung the Westminster quarters every quarter-hour since New Year's Day 1928, a signature sound of the city. The 1923 HSBC Building and the 1929 Sassoon House, today the Peace Hotel, are likewise landmarks of the street. Across the river in Lujiazui, Shanghai Tower (632 m), Shanghai World Financial Center (492 m) and Jin Mao Tower (420.5 m) form the trio skyline, and together with the Bund's historic buildings they witness a century of change along the former concession waterfront. The Bund promenade and flood wall have been rebuilt several times and are open free along the river; they are the first choice as a starting point for a night cruise or for photography.

Shanghai Museum

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

The Shanghai Museum was founded in 1952. Its People's Square building is known for its collection of ancient Chinese art, with more than a million objects. The bronze, ceramics, painting and jade galleries are the most representative permanent displays: the Da Ke ding, the Jin Hou Su bells, Yue ware mise porcelain and Tang and Song paintings are all shown here. The East Branch, opened in 2024 in Huamu, Pudong, has larger exhibition spaces, creating a two-building museum of the People's Square building and the East Branch. A temple of ancient Chinese art beside People's Square, it is especially strong in bronzes, ceramics, painting and jade. With the new East Branch open, the exhibition space is larger, and it is the place most worth half a day for understanding Jiangnan culture and Chinese art. Tip: the People's Square building and the East Branch are booked separately, so do not go to the wrong one; allow at least three hours. Practical info: the museum is at 201 People's Avenue, Huangpu District, Shanghai; admission is free (reservation required, some special exhibitions ticketed separately); opening hours 09:00–17:00 (closed Mondays, last entry 16:00).

Oriental Pearl Radio and TV Tower

★★★★★Recommend 4.6 / 5

The Oriental Pearl Radio and Television Tower stands on the tip of Lujiazui where the land reaches into the Huangpu River. The tower is 468 metres tall and made of 11 spheres of different sizes strung along its shaft; it serves both broadcasting and sightseeing, was described at its opening as the second tallest tower in Asia and fourth in the world, and was the first high viewing point in Pudong open to the public. The viewing spaces are arranged in levels: the upper sphere observation deck at 263 metres looks 360 degrees down on both banks of the Huangpu; the 259-metre skywalk projects a transparent glass walkway outside the tower shaft; the upper sphere at 267 metres holds a revolving restaurant; and the 350-metre space module is the higher observation level. On the ground floor of the base, the Shanghai Urban History Exhibition Hall uses reconstructed scenes to tell the city's story since the port opened, dovetailing neatly with a tower visit. Highlights include the 263-metre upper-sphere observation deck, the 259-metre transparent skywalk, the 267-metre revolving restaurant and the Shanghai Urban History Exhibition Hall at the tower base. Tip: the two-sphere and three-sphere tickets cover different levels — check whether yours includes the 350-metre space module. Practical info: the tower is at 1 Century Avenue, Pudong New Area, Shanghai; the two-sphere ticket is ¥160; opening hours 08:00–21:30.

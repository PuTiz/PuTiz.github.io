City Intro

History & Culture: Changsha, also called Star City, lies on the lower Xiang River in the Chang-Liu Basin. It is the capital of Hunan Province and a national historical and cultural city. The astonishing discoveries at the Mawangdui Han tombs and the thousand-year scholarly tradition of Yuelu Academy testify to the depth of Huxiang culture. Changsha is a city where historical depth and trendy street life intertwine. Huxiang culture has a long history: Yuelu Academy's scholarly tradition has never broken, the Mawangdui Han tombs astonished the world, and the Mao Zedong Youth Statue stands by the river at Orange Island Head. In recent years Changsha has become an internet-famous city through Super Wenheyou, Chayan Yuese and the nightlife of Jiefang West, fusing weighty history with youthful energy. Events such as the Changsha International Marathon give this ancient city a fresh vitality.

Geography: Located on the lower Xiang River in the Chang-Liu Basin, Changsha has a subtropical monsoon climate — warm and humid with four distinct seasons. The Mao Zedong Youth Statue stands by the river at Orange Island Head; the Xiang flows north and Yuelu Mountain screens the city to the west, giving a distinctive mountain-river-island-city layout. Changsha sits in Hunan Province, where landforms, rivers and climate have together shaped the city's spatial pattern. Spring (Mar–May) brings lush flowers and trees; autumn (Sep–Nov) is clear and fresh — the most pleasant times. Natural and cultural sites are fairly concentrated, so a one- or two-day itinerary by area works well. Light, vegetation and water levels change with the seasons; check the weather and official notices before setting out. Natural and cultural sites around the city can usually be linked by road or rail, which suits a half-day or full-day outing.

Highlights: Climb Yuelu Mountain, visit Orange Island, see the Mawangdui treasures and taste stinky tofu, experiencing the historical depth and trendy bustle of this internet-famous city. Core sights include Yuelu Mountain and Yuelu Academy, Orange Island, Hunan Museum and Tianxin Pavilion. They cover history, nature and city life, and can be combined freely. Pair the main sights with nearby streets, museums or food streets for a half- or full-day route. If time allows, slow down and leave room for walking and meals. First-time visitors can start with the most representative sights, then add museums, old streets or nature as time allows. Local food is part of the itinerary — save a meal for a time-honoured shop or a local market.

Food Culture: Changsha's food is boldly hot: stinky tofu is crisp outside and tender inside, chopped-chilli fish head is salty, spicy and savoury, kouwei crawfish is numbing and irresistible, and sugar-oil baba is sweet without cloying. The scent of Chayan Yuese drifts through the streets.

Yuelu Mountain and Yuelu Academy

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Yuelu Mountain is the last of the 72 peaks of Mount Heng, the Southern Sacred Mountain, rising to 300.8 metres, and has been a gathering place of culture since ancient times. Yuelu Academy was founded in 976, the ninth year of the Kaibao era of the Northern Song, and is one of China's four great ancient academies, its scholarly tradition unbroken for a thousand years. Aiwan Pavilion stands halfway up the mountain; its name comes from Du Mu's line about stopping the carriage to enjoy the maple grove at dusk, and in autumn the maples blaze like fire. Yuelu Mountain is thickly wooded; Yuelu Academy in its folds is one of the four great academies of the Northern Song, a thousand-year seat of learning with an unbroken tradition, and Aiwan Pavilion is famous for its maple leaves. Highlights include Lushan Temple and Yunlu Palace. Tip: Autumn, when the maples turn, is the most beautiful season. Practical info: located at Lushan South Road, Yuelu District, Changsha, Hunan; Yuelu Mountain free, the academy ¥50; open 07:00–18:00. Best season: spring (Mar–May) brings lush flowers and trees; autumn (Sep–Nov) is clear and fresh — the most pleasant times. Combine with Orange Island, Hunan Museum and Tianxin Pavilion for a half-day or full-day itinerary.

  • Address: Lushan South Road, Yuelu District, Changsha, Hunan
  • Ticket: Yuelu Mountain free; academy ¥50
  • Opening: 07:00–18:00

Orange Island

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

Orange Island is an island in the Xiang River, about 5 kilometres long, famous because Mao Zedong studied and swam here in his youth. At its head stands the Mao Zedong Youth Art Sculpture, 32 metres tall, the island's signature landmark. Highlights include the Orange Island Head viewing platform, Beach Park and Wentian Terrace. A long, narrow island in the middle of the Xiang River. At its head stands the monumental Mao Zedong Youth Art Sculpture, the signature landmark of Changsha's mountain-river-island cityscape. Tip: Take the sightseeing train to the island head; walking there and back takes about 2–3 hours. Practical info: located at Orange Island Head, Yuelu District, Changsha, Hunan; admission free (sightseeing train ¥20); open 07:00–22:00. Best season: spring (Mar–May) brings lush flowers and trees; autumn (Sep–Nov) is clear and fresh — the most pleasant times. Combine with Yuelu Mountain and Yuelu Academy, Hunan Museum and Tianxin Pavilion for a half-day or full-day itinerary. Confirm opening hours and reservation policies before you go, and allow plenty of time.

  • Address: Orange Island Head, Yuelu District, Changsha, Hunan
  • Ticket: Free (sightseeing train ¥20)
  • Opening: 07:00–22:00

Hunan Museum

★★★★★Recommend 4.8 / 5

Hunan Museum (formerly Hunan Provincial Museum) is among the first batch of national first-class museums, with more than 180,000 artefacts; the Mawangdui Han tomb finds are the most striking. Lady Dai's body is remarkably preserved after more than 2,000 years, the gossamer-thin silk robe weighs only 49 grams, and the T-shaped silk banner retains colours as vivid as new — a premier place for understanding Han dynasty civilisation. Highlights include the Mawangdui Han Tombs exhibition. A national first-class museum whose Mawangdui Han tomb finds — including Lady Dai's preserved body and the gossamer-thin silk robe — astonished the world, it is a premier place to explore Han dynasty civilisation. Tip: Book in advance; allow about 2–3 hours. Practical info: located at 50 Dongfeng Road, Kaifu District, Changsha, Hunan; admission free (reservation required); open 09:00–17:00 (closed Mondays). Best season: spring (Mar–May) brings lush flowers and trees; autumn (Sep–Nov) is clear and fresh — the most pleasant times. Combine with Yuelu Mountain and Yuelu Academy, Orange Island and Tianxin Pavilion for a half-day or full-day itinerary.

Tip: Book in advance and allow about 2–3 hours. The Mawangdui galleries are the must-see.

  • Address: 50 Dongfeng Road, Kaifu District, Changsha, Hunan
  • Ticket: Free (reservation required)
  • Opening: 09:00–17:00 (closed Mondays)

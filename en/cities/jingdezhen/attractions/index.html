Jingdezhen Ancient Kiln Folk Customs Museum

★★★★★Recommend 4.6 / 5

Jingdezhen Ancient Kiln Folk Customs Museum is the country's only national 5A-level scenic area devoted to ceramic culture. Kilns of the Song, Yuan, Ming and Qing dynasties have been reconstructed here, and visitors can watch the seventy-two steps of hand-made porcelain, including throwing, trimming, blue-and-white painting and glazing, and feel the craft heritage of the thousand-year porcelain capital. Highlights include the Song dynasty dragon kiln, the Ming dynasty gourd kiln, the Qing dynasty town kiln and hand-making demonstrations. The country's only national 5A scenic area devoted to ceramic culture, it gathers reconstructions of kilns from successive dynasties, where you can watch throwing, blue-and-white painting and other hand-making skills, while the imperial kiln site recalls the glory of porcelain made for the court. Tip: go in the morning to see the full porcelain-making demonstrations. Practical info: located at 1 Guyao Road, Cidu Avenue, Changjiang District, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi; ticket ¥95; opening hours 08:00–17:30. Best season: spring (Mar–May) and autumn (Sep–Nov) are mild and ideal for visiting kilns and admiring porcelain; summer is hotter but the ceramic markets are lively.

Tip: Go in the morning to see the full porcelain-making demonstrations. The site is large; allow 3–4 hours.

  • Address: 1 Guyao Road, Cidu Avenue, Changjiang District, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi
  • Ticket: ¥95
  • Opening: 08:00–17:30

Taoxichuan Cultural and Creative Block

★★★★☆Recommend 4.5 / 5

Taoxichuan was converted from the former state-run Yuzhou Porcelain Factory, retaining the red-brick structure and chimneys of the old workshops while incorporating modern design. It has a ceramic art museum, hand-making experience shops and a creative market, and is a gathering place for Jingdezhen's young ceramic artists. Highlights include the weekend creative market, hand-made porcelain experiences and popular photo spots. A creative landmark converted from an old porcelain factory, where red-brick workshops meet galleries and markets, it has a lively night market and is a great place for young visitors to hunt for handmade ceramics and see the porcelain capital's new face. Tip: the creative market runs on Friday to Sunday evenings and is very lively. Practical info: located at 150 Xinchang West Road, Zhushan District, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi; free entry; open all day (markets mostly at night). Best season: spring (Mar–May) and autumn (Sep–Nov) are mild and ideal for visiting kilns and admiring porcelain; summer is hotter but the ceramic markets are lively. Combine with Jingdezhen Ancient Kiln Folk Customs Museum, Yaoli Ancient Town and the Imperial Kiln National Archaeological Site Park in Jingdezhen for a half-day or full-day itinerary.

Tip: The creative market runs on Friday to Sunday evenings and is very lively.

  • Address: 150 Xinchang West Road, Zhushan District, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi
  • Ticket: Free
  • Opening: Open all day (markets mostly at night)

China Ceramic Museum

★★★★★Recommend 4.7 / 5

China Ceramic Museum stands in Changjiang District, Jingdezhen, and opened in 2004 as the country's first large museum devoted to ceramics. Its collection of ceramic samples and vessels numbers in the tens of thousands, and permanent displays follow a timeline from Neolithic pottery and Han and Tang kilns through Song and Yuan blue-and-white and celadon to Ming and Qing imperial ware and modern ceramics, systematically presenting the history of Chinese ceramics and Jingdezhen's kiln industry. Blue-and-white — including a plum vase with scrolling lotus — famille rose, monochrome glazes and kiln samples from successive periods are the focus. The country's first large museum devoted to ceramics, it runs from Neolithic pottery through Ming and Qing imperial ware to modern ceramic art; blue-and-white, famille rose, monochrome glazes and kiln samples from successive periods are the focus, and it is free with reservation — the place to understand Jingdezhen's status as porcelain capital. Highlights include the general history of ceramics and blue-and-white and famille rose. Tip: see the general history galleries first, then Ming and Qing imperial ware and contemporary ceramics; it is on the same line as the imperial kiln and Taoxichuan — allow at least 2 hours. Practical info: located at 1 Zijing North Road, Changjiang District, Jingdezhen; free entry (reservation required); opening hours 09:00–17:00 (closed Mondays, last entry 16:30).

Yaoli Ancient Town

★★★★☆Recommend 4.4 / 5

Yaoli was anciently written with the characters for 'kiln village', a name it took from the many kilns here since the Song and Yuan dynasties. Forest coverage in the town exceeds 86.3%, and it is a Chinese Historical and Cultural Town and a national key scenic area; ancient kilns and water-mill remains survive around Raonan, and Ming and Qing shops line both banks of the Yao River. The Yaoli scenic area also takes in the Gaoling porcelain-clay mine site and the ancient Dongbu wharf: the 'binary formula' of adding Gaoling clay to porcelain stone appeared here more than seven hundred years ago, making it possible to fire large vessels at high temperature, and the mineralogical term kaolin comes from the name of Gaoling mountain. In July 2026, the 'Jingdezhen handmade porcelain industry remains' were inscribed on the World Heritage list as 'one town, four districts', and the Gaoling site is among them; the town also preserves the site of the Yaoli reorganisation of the New Fourth Army, led by Chen Yi in January 1938. Highlights include the Ming and Qing old street, the Raonan ancient kiln site and tea culture experiences. Tip: it is about 50 km from the city; hiring a car is recommended. Practical info: located at Yaoli Town, Fuliang County, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi; free entry (combo ticket for some sights ¥150); opening hours 08:00–17:30.

Tip: It is about 50 km from the city; hiring a car is recommended.

  • Address: Yaoli Town, Fuliang County, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi
  • Ticket: Free (combo ticket for some sights ¥150)
  • Opening: 08:00–17:30

Imperial Kiln National Archaeological Site Park

★★★★☆Recommend 4.4 / 5

The imperial kiln was the royal factory that fired porcelain for the court in the Ming and Qing dynasties; the site covers about 50,000 square metres. Excavations have yielded large numbers of precious fragments of official kiln porcelain, and the Dragon Pearl Pavilion is the site's landmark. Highlights include the archaeological remains, displays of official kiln porcelain and Ming and Qing kiln remains. The Ming and Qing imperial kiln site, its ground packed with fragments of rejected official porcelain, with the Dragon Pearl Pavilion standing tall, is the key place to learn about Jingdezhen's imperial kiln system and its archaeology. Tip: entry is free, and a guide will deepen your understanding of the imperial kiln's history. Practical info: located at Zhushan Middle Road, Zhushan District, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi; free entry; opening hours 09:00–17:00. Best season: spring (Mar–May) and autumn (Sep–Nov) are mild and ideal for visiting kilns and admiring porcelain; summer is hotter but the ceramic markets are lively. Combine with Jingdezhen Ancient Kiln Folk Customs Museum, Taoxichuan Cultural and Creative Block and Yaoli Ancient Town in Jingdezhen for a half-day or full-day itinerary.

Tip: Entry is free, and a guide will deepen your understanding of the imperial kiln's history.

  • Address: Zhushan Middle Road, Zhushan District, Jingdezhen, Jiangxi
  • Ticket: Free
  • Opening: 09:00–17:00
